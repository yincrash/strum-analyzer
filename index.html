Tips
  • Hold the phone or laptop mic a few cm from the belt and pluck it like a guitar string.
  • If the level meter never turns green, lower the Gate slider. If it is always green from room noise, raise it. The gate is never allowed within 8 dB of the measured noise floor, and a pluck less than 12 dB above the floor is marked weak. Turn off fans and air conditioning while measuring: a pluck should stand 20 dB or more above the room.
  • Clarity near 1.0 means a clean tone. Belt plucks often ring for only 100–200 ms, so clarity of 0.6–0.8 is normal. Readings with clarity below the Min clarity slider fall back to the strongest spectral peak.
  • You can also pick a voice memo or other recording with Analyze a recording. Each pluck in the file is listed in the history and the loudest one's spectrum is drawn.
  • 60 Hz mains hum has a harmonic at 120 Hz. If you see a steady 120 Hz with nothing plucked, that is hum, not the belt. Move away from power supplies or fans.
  • Presets come from each printer's public docs and are only starting points. Check your own manual, and note that the frequency depends on where you pluck and how long the free span is.
  • Every pluck lists its prominent peaks. Harmonics of the reading are labeled 2×, 3×. A higher peak that is not a harmonic is called out as "also N Hz": that is a second ringing part (the other belt, a longer span, the frame). If that is the belt, it is tighter than the reading says, so look at the spectrum before tightening further. Tones that were already present before the pluck (hum, fans) are ignored automatically.
  • When listening starts, the status line reports whether the browser honored the request for raw audio. Phone browsers often keep automatic gain control or noise suppression on, which lowers and flattens plucks. A dedicated recording app with processing disabled usually gives a cleaner signal; analyze its files with Analyze a recording.
  • Record 10 s & analyze captures the page's own microphone input and runs it through the same analysis used for files, and offers the recording to save. Use it to compare against a recording app: pluck while both are running and feed the app's file to Analyze a recording.
  • The FFT peak reading is a second opinion. If it disagrees by a factor of 2 the detector may be picking an octave; trust the one nearest the strongest spectrum bump.
